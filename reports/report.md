# Evidence-Backed Support Agent for @SpotifyCares

*Hiver SDE Intern take-home. Built in a 2-hour budget. Every number below is
regenerated by `python -m src.evaluation`; none are transcribed by hand.*

---

## 1. Problem framing — what "good" means here, and what I chose not to build

@SpotifyCares answers public tweets. A wrong answer is not a silent error: it is
visible, quotable, and attached to the brand. So the objective is **not** to
maximise how many tweets the bot answers. It is:

> Answer the messages we can answer *with evidence*, hand over everything else
> with a stated reason, and make both outcomes auditable by a human in seconds.

That gives three quality bars, kept separate throughout:

| Level | Question | Where measured |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Is the intent right? | `reports/evaluation.md` §1 |
| Agent | Was auto-handle-vs-escalate the right call? | §2 |
| Response | Is the drafted reply usable? | §3 + `human_agreement.md` |

**What I deliberately did not build**, and why:

- **An autonomous LLM agent that plans its own tool use.** τ-bench (Yao et al., 2024) measured frontier function-calling agents on exactly this shape of task: <50% success and `pass^8` under 25% on retail. An escalation path that behaves differently on retry is worse than no automation.
- **A fine-tuned transformer classifier.** The intent signal here is overwhelmingly lexical. A linear model gives per-word coefficients I can show a reviewer, 5ms inference, no GPU, and a 15-minute reproduction.
- **Multi-turn conversation modelling.** Real gap, and I measured its size rather than hand-waving: 50.5% of customer messages trip no intent rule at all, many being context-dependent follow-ups (`"ok will try"`, `"galaxy s5"`). Fixing it properly needs thread-state modelling — a one-week item, not a two-hour one.
- **Multilingual support.** 145 non-English rows dropped rather than mishandled.
- **A vector database.** 13.9k documents. `linear_kernel` on a sparse matrix is faster than the network hop to a vector DB would be.

## 2. Selected brand: `SpotifyCares`

Chosen by scoring the ten highest-volume brands, not by picking the biggest
(`reports/brand_selection.csv`, regenerated by `src/data_processing.py`).

| brand | answered pairs | boilerplate replies | median reply chars | English share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AmazonHelp | 168,814 | 0.6% | 120 | 0.926 |
| AppleSupport | 106,646 | **38.9%** | 129 | 0.969 |
| Uber_Support | 56,160 | 31.3% | 104 | 0.995 |
| **SpotifyCares** | **43,092** | **25.7%** | **131** | **0.983** |
| Delta | 42,114 | 14.0% | 102 | 0.985 |
| TMobileHelp | 34,215 | 60.6% | 126 | 0.987 |

Volume alone is the wrong criterion. The system grounds replies in historical
resolutions, so what matters is whether the brand's replies *contain* a
resolution:

- **AmazonHelp** has 4× the volume but is heavily multilingual (Japanese threads throughout) — it would need language ID before anything else works.
- **AppleSupport** sends 39% pure hand-off replies ("DM us"), which gives retrieval little to ground on.
- **TMobileHelp** is 61% boilerplate.
- **SpotifyCares** hits the balance: 43k answered pairs (we model 20k), 98% English, and replies with actual steps — *"tap the three dots > View Album"*, *"best thing to try here is a reinstall"*. It also has a genuine high-risk subset (billing, cancellation, account takeover) that makes the escalation policy meaningful rather than decorative.

**Sampling**: 43,092 answered pairs → filters → 20,000 pairs across 13,637
conversations, sampled **by whole conversation** with `seed=42`.

## 3. Data processing and leakage control

Pairs are built on the reply edge (brand tweet → `in_response_to_tweet_id` →
customer tweet), so every training row has a real historical resolution attached.

| step | rows |
|---|---|
| raw answered pairs | 43,092 |
| after null drop | 43,092 |
| dropped: length outside 15–1200 chars | 791 |
| dropped: duplicate messages | 1,964 |
| dropped: non-English | 145 |
| **final modelling set** | **20,000** (13,637 conversations) |

Splits: train 13,891 / val 3,033 / test 3,076.

**Leakage control** — Kapoor & Narayanan (2023) catalogue leakage as the single
most common cause of irreproducible ML results. `conversation_id` (the root of
each reply chain) is the grouping key **everywhere**:

1. Train/val/test via `GroupShuffleSplit`.
2. **Inside** hyperparameter search via `GroupKFold` — a plain `KFold` would have tuned on leaked folds.
3. The calibration/threshold halves of validation are conversation-disjoint from each other.
4. **The retrieval index contains training rows only** — otherwise a test message retrieves *itself* at similarity 1.0 and every evidence-quality number becomes fiction.

All four are enforced by `tests/test_pipeline.py`, not by good intentions. 18 tests pass.

## 4. Intent taxonomy

Nine intents derived from the brand's own traffic by sampling and inspection, not
invented a priori. Labels come from weak supervision (Snorkel-style labelling
functions, Ratner et al. 2017) — 9 rule groups, weighted vote.

| intent | risk | share | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `general_complaint_feedback` | LOW | 72.2% | vague venting / praise, no specific fault |
| `subscription_plan` | MEDIUM | 8.5% | *"I paid for Premium but it still shows Free"* |
| `app_device_bug` | MEDIUM | 4.3% | *"crashes on my Xbox since the update"* |
| `feature_how_to` | LOW | 3.6% | *"is there a way to see my listening history?"* |
| `content_availability` | LOW | 3.2% | *"why isn't this album available in my country"* |
| `account_login_access` | **HIGH** | 3.1% | *"can't log in, reset email never arrives"* |
| `playback_streaming_issue` | MEDIUM | 2.1% | *"downloaded songs won't play offline"* |
| `billing_payment` | **HIGH** | 1.8% | *"charged me twice, want a refund"* |
| `cancellation_request` | **HIGH** | 1.2% | *"how do I cancel premium"* |

**The 72% catch-all is the most important fact about this dataset.** My first
taxonomy put 79% there; inspecting the bucket showed a real rule-coverage gap
(*"y isnt spotify available in south africa"*, *"i dont remember my email for my
premium account"*), so I broadened the labelling functions and it fell to 72.2%.
The residual is largely genuine: **50.5% of messages trip no rule at all**,
because a large fraction of tweets in a thread are context-dependent follow-ups
with no standalone intent. That drives the headline-number discussion in §10.

## 5. Features, scaling, model

**Text**: TF-IDF word 1–2 grams (topical signal) + char_wb 3–4 grams (the Twitter
insurance policy — matches `cancle`, `playlst`, hashtag mashups that a word
vectoriser turns into unseen vocabulary).

**Numeric (11)**: char/word/sentence counts, `?` and `!` counts, uppercase ratio,
digit ratio, URL flag, mention count, hashtag count, lexicon sentiment. These run
on the **raw** text, because case and punctuation *are* the signal — `"WTF IS
GOING ON"` is a different message from `"wtf is going on"` and cleaning would
destroy exactly what they measure.

**Scaling**: `StandardScaler` on the 11 dense numeric columns **only**.
Standardising sparse TF-IDF would subtract the mean from every zero and densify a
20k × 200k matrix for no accuracy gain; TF-IDF rows are already L2-normalised, so
both blocks reach the classifier on comparable scales. Fused in one
`ColumnTransformer` inside one `Pipeline`, so vocabulary and scaler statistics are
fitted on the training fold only — including inside cross-validation.

**Model**: multinomial logistic regression. Chosen over LinearSVC because the
decision layer needs probabilities to threshold on, and SVC would need a
`CalibratedClassifierCV` wrapper for the same thing at 3× the fits.

## 6. Hyperparameter optimisation

`GridSearchCV`, 12 configs × 3-fold `GroupKFold` = 36 fits, scoring `f1_macro`,
**on the training split only** — the test set is touched exactly once, at the end.

```json
{"clf__C": "10.0", "clf__class_weight": "balanced",
 "features__word__ngram_range": "(1, 2)"}
```

Best CV macro-F1 **0.7183**, search time 110s. `class_weight="balanced"` was the
single biggest lever, which is what a 58× imbalance ratio predicts.

## 7. Results vs baselines

Held-out test set, 3,076 rows, weak-supervision labels:

| model | accuracy | macro F1 | weighted F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline 1 — majority class | 0.720 | **0.093** | 0.603 |
| Baseline 2 — TF-IDF unigram + LogReg | 0.813 | 0.544 | 0.790 |
| Baseline 3 — TF-IDF + ComplementNB | 0.786 | 0.528 | 0.767 |
| Ablation — word TF-IDF only, tuned params | 0.841 | **0.721** | 0.847 |
| **Tuned full model (shipped)** | **0.842** | **0.711** | **0.848** |

Macro-F1 **0.544 → 0.711 (+31%)** over the simple baseline.

**An honest result I am not hiding:** the ablation *beats* the shipped model on
macro F1 (0.721 vs 0.711). The char n-grams and numeric features **did not earn
their place** on this data. The shipped model is the grid-search winner, selected
by cross-validation without touching test — but a cleaner experiment would have
put the feature-block toggle *inside* the CV grid rather than evaluating it as an
afterthought on test. That is a methodology fix, not a tuning fix, and it is the
first thing I would correct.

Note also how baseline 1 behaves: **72% accuracy, 0.093 macro F1**. One number
says "decent", the other says "useless". That gap is the whole argument for macro
F1 as the model-selection metric here.

## 8. The agent, and why it is hybrid

```
CUSTOMER MESSAGE
      |
      v
 [1] classify_intent      ML    calibrated probabilities, top-2 margin
 [2] assess_risk          RULE  risk level + hard blocks (on the RAW message)
 [3] retrieve_similar     ML    TF-IDF cosine over 13,891 training cases
 [4] validate_evidence    RULE  5-signal evidence-quality score + flags
      |
      v
 [5] determine_action     RULE  selective-prediction gate -> AUTO-HANDLE | ESCALATE
      |
      v
 [6] generate_reply       LLM   grounded draft (or extractive fallback)
 [7] critique_reply       RULE  deterministic checks; may only tighten the decision
      |
      v
 EVIDENCE-BACKED SUPPORT DECISION  (+ full replayable trace)
```

**Responsibility split — the core design claim:**

| Layer | Owns | Cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Agent** | tool order, state, error handling, final decision | — |
| **ML** | intent probabilities, calibration, similarity | set policy |
| **Rules** | risk level, hard blocks, thresholds, forbidden commitments | be overridden by the LLM |
| **LLM** | reply wording, advisory critique | change the decision, lower risk, bypass a block |

The tool *order* is fixed rather than model-planned because every case needs the
same four facts before a decision is possible, and because τ-bench showed the
cost of letting a model decide that: `pass^8 < 25%`.

**Escalation is abstention** (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017). A message is auto-handled
only if *all* gates pass: no hard block, confidence ≥ threshold, top-2 margin ≥
threshold, evidence quality ≥ threshold, no evidence flags, and the intent is not
HIGH risk. Thresholds are **not hand-picked** — they come from a
(confidence × evidence) sweep on validation, choosing max coverage subject to
selective accuracy ≥ 90% (`reports/risk_coverage_sweep.csv`).

**Calibration, reported honestly:** Platt scaling moved ECE from 0.0290 to 0.0290
— i.e. **it did nothing**. The finding is not "calibration fixed our
probabilities"; it is that a regularised linear model was already near-calibrated,
which is a different regime from the deep networks Guo et al. studied. I ran the
check instead of assuming either way, and kept the calibrator only because it is
marginally better.

## 9. Retrieval, grounding, and the trust panel

TF-IDF cosine over training customer messages, returning the brand's actual
replies with similarity scores and provenance. It works well on the cases that
matter — *"charged twice for premium"* retrieves real billing precedents at
similarity 0.49/0.46/0.46.

**Grounding is enforced, not requested.** The generator sees only the message,
intent, precedents, and policy constraints — it has no other source from which to
invent a policy. Then `critic.py` checks the draft *externally*: forbidden-commitment
regexes (refund promises, invented timelines, credential requests), lexical
overlap with the precedents, and URL provenance (any link not in the evidence is
an invented link → escalate). Following Huang et al. (2024), **no verdict-changing
check asks the LLM to grade itself**.

Every response exposes the full **Evidence-Backed Support Decision**: intent,
confidence, runner-up + margin, risk, evidence quality with its five sub-signals,
decision, reason, the precedents with similarity scores, the critic verdict, and
the step-by-step trace.

## 10. What is misleading about my headline number

**Headline: 84.2% accuracy / 0.711 macro F1.** Six reasons that is softer than it
looks, roughly in order of severity:

1. **The labels are rules, so the model is partly graded by its own teacher.** Training labels come from regex labelling functions. A TF-IDF model trained on them re-learns those keywords, then gets tested against the same keywords. This is not a small effect — it is the single biggest reason to distrust 84.2%. The golden set exists precisely to break the loop, which is why **it must be human-reviewed before its number means anything**.
2. **A 72% catch-all class inflates accuracy directly.** Predicting `general_complaint_feedback` for everything scores 72%. Most of the apparent skill is in the majority class; macro F1 (0.711) is the honest summary, and even that is propped up by classes the rules define crisply.
3. **The escalation gate is graded against the same weak labels.** "90% selective accuracy" from the threshold sweep means 90% agreement *with rules*, not with a human.
4. **The golden set is deliberately harder than production traffic.** It over-samples ambiguous, short, long, low-confidence and high-risk cases. Its score is a conservative floor, not a production estimate — and comparing it to the test number without that caveat would be apples-to-oranges in the flattering direction.
5. **Classification accuracy says nothing about reply quality.** The agent can classify `billing_payment` perfectly and still draft something useless. That is why response quality is measured separately — and why, with no LLM key configured, the reply layer is currently *extractive* and its "grounding 5.0/5.0" score is close to tautological: reusing a precedent verbatim is trivially grounded in it.
6. **Single-turn framing mismatches the data.** 50.5% of messages trip no rule, many being follow-ups (`"ok will try"`) whose intent lives in the preceding turn. The model assigns them a label anyway. Accuracy on such rows is close to meaningless in either direction.

**The number I would actually defend** is not accuracy. It is: *of the 46% of
messages the agent chose to answer alone, 85.9% had the right intent* — with the
other 54% routed to a human with a written reason. And even that carries caveat 1
until the golden set is reviewed.

## 11. Failure analysis — top 5 modes, real rows only

From `reports/agent_decisions_golden.csv`. 37 of 200 golden messages misclassified.

| # | mode | n | real example | expected → predicted | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Missed a specific intent** | 24 | *"Ok, 2018 Skoda Kodiaq, Columbus Big Navi, CarPlay"* | `app_device_bug` → `general_complaint_feedback` (0.94) | Pure device context, no fault words. Intent lives in the previous turn. **Worst case here: it was AUTO-HANDLED at 0.94 confidence** — high confidence on a message with no standalone intent. |
| 2 | **Label is wrong, model is right** | 6 | *"I deleted my Facebook account and I can no longer login... I refuse to reactivate"* | `general_complaint_feedback` → `account_login_access` (0.96) | The *rule* label is wrong; the model is right. Direct evidence for §10.1 — and it counts against the model in the metric. |
| 3 | **Overlapping intents** | 4 | *"on the app it says PREMIUM TRIAL but the website says it'll renew next month... I want to c[ancel]"* | `cancellation_request` → `subscription_plan` (0.92) | Genuinely both. Escalated correctly (evidence quality 0.21). |
| 4 | **Low-confidence confusion** | 3 | *"premium acc. Searching for album... Green button is shuffle play. How do I play all tracks?"* | `subscription_plan` → `feature_how_to` (0.44) | Two topics in one tweet. Escalated correctly. |
| 5 | **Boilerplate-only precedent** | — | *"charged me twice for premium, please refund"* | intent correct (0.97) | Classification fine; **retrieval** returns only "DM us" replies, so `precedent_is_boilerplate_only` fires → escalate. The right call, for the right reason. |

Modes 3–5 are the system **working**: it escalated. Mode 1 is the real defect —
confident auto-handling of context-free follow-ups. Mode 2 is a labelling defect,
not a model defect.

## 13. What I would do with one more week

Ranked by risk reduction per unit of effort:

1. **Review the golden set + a second annotator** (½ day). Everything else is measured against it. Without it there is no trustworthy number, and a second annotator gives inter-annotator agreement to bound how good the judge could possibly look.
2. **Put the feature-block choice inside the CV grid** (1 hour). Fixes the §7 methodology gap directly.
3. **Thread-context features** (1–2 days). Highest-impact model change: failure mode 1 is 65% of all errors and is caused by single-turn framing. Previous-turn intent + turn index should fix most of it.
4. **NLI-based grounding** (1 day). Replace lexical overlap with entailment against the precedents — the current proxy over-credits copying, which is exactly what the extractive fallback does.
5. **Embedding retrieval + measured comparison** (1 day). The harness is in place; the point is to *prove* dense beats sparse here, not assume it.
6. **Learned escalation policy** (2 days). Once reviewer accept/edit/reject decisions are logged, train the gate on real outcomes instead of proxy signals.
7. **Human feedback loop + drift monitoring** (2 days). Log every override; alert on intent-distribution shift. The Evidence Panel already captures the state needed — it is the foundation for this, not a demo feature.
